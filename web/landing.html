The number

The gap is architectural, not a faster provider.

The pipeline is sequential — there is no concurrency to win here. Each scene is segmented and pushed to B2 as soon as it exists, so playback starts on scene 1 instead of on scene N. The app measures both clocks itself and writes them into the job record.

Across the six pipeline runs recorded in data/firstframe.db, first frame lands between 5.4 s and 10.9 s against 22.8 s and 36.5 s of full render — a 2.5× to 4.5× gap. The clock is not decorative: it stops on the first segment of real content, and the LIVE slate is explicitly excluded from it.

What it does

Four things, all of them visible in the product.

Stream

Review starts on scene one

Every scene is encoded into 2-second HLS segments and uploaded the instant it lands. The player attaches to a playlist that is still growing.

Iterate

Reject at second three

Request changes on any scene and the AgentLoop re-runs it through a vision judge. The refined take joins the same playlist — no reload, no second link.

Prove

A manifest you can verify

Each job seals a manifest with the Genblaze version, bucket, region, per-scene lineage and canonical hashes. Check it with genblaze verify.

Keep

Approved means locked

Approving copies the spot into approved/ under S3 Object Lock in GOVERNANCE mode. Nobody deletes or overwrites it for 30 days.

Custody

Approve it and it stops being deletable.

The moment a reviewer approves, the object moves to the approved prefix with a retention date on it. The panel shows the lock state next to the player, not in a log file.

  • Object Lock GOVERNANCE, retain until a date the manifest records.
  • 30 days of retention on every approved spot.
  • Bucket genblaze-review-migarci2, region eu-central-003.
